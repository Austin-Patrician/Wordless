A digital employee is not a different model name. It is a **reusable session role**: a name, description, portrait, instructions, and explicitly enabled Skills and MCP. Summoning an employee creates a new session and stores the role configuration as that session’s snapshot.

<OperationalBlueprint kind="employees" locale="en" />

## Choose the right shape

| Shape | Use it for | Accountability |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Individual employee | A stable writing, research, review, or execution role | The role works in the primary session; you still verify the result |
| Employee team | Work with separable research, review, synthesis, or independent perspectives | The Team Lead owns the main conversation, delegation decisions, conflicts, and final delivery |

A team has two to nine members. The lead is not an “extra powerful member”; the lead is the coordinator in the primary session. Other members run only when the lead delegates a task.

## Create an individual employee

Open **Digital Employees** in the left rail, stay on the **Employees** tab, and choose `+`.

<Steps>
1. Enter the name, description, and portrait. The portrait identifies the role in cards, sessions, and rosters; it does not change model capability.
2. In **Employee instructions**, state the identity, responsibility, input boundary, output format, prohibited actions, and evidence requirements. Keep durable rules here and one-off outcomes in the summon prompt.
3. Select only the Skills and MCP this role actually needs. Connector availability still follows the current configuration and approval policy.
4. Save, then choose **Summon** from the card or detail view. Suggested prompts in the detail view can be sent as the initial task.
</Steps>

<PromptBlock label="Employee instruction example">You are a product researcher. Separate verified facts, inference, and unknowns. When using external material, preserve the URL, date, and source name. Produce a competitor comparison table, evidence gaps, and three recommendations for the product owner. Never present an uncited market-size claim as fact, and never modify workspace files unless the user explicitly asks in the current session.</PromptBlock>

The employee configuration enters a new session as a snapshot. Editing an employee later does not rewrite a session that has already started; summon it again to use the new instructions. Before deleting a role or removing it from a team, check which sessions and teams still reference it.

## Build an employee team

Switch to the **Teams** tab and choose `+`. The team editor separates team information from the member roster:

<Steps>
1. Enter the team name, description, and team instructions. Describe the shared outcome, delivery standard, delegation criteria, and how the lead should synthesize member results.
2. Add at least two members, or copy existing employees. Every member needs a name, responsibility, and member instructions; a team can contain up to nine members.
3. Choose a Team Lead. The lead owns the main conversation, task scheduling, and final response. Changing the lead sets its execution boundary to `workspace-write`.
4. Choose a profile for collaborators: `read-only`, `review`, `research`, or `workspace-write`. Only non-lead members can set a separate model and thinking depth; leaving them unset follows the primary session.
5. Save and summon the team. When delegation is worthwhile, the lead calls `delegate_expert` and chooses single, parallel, or sequential work based on task dependencies.
</Steps>

## Member execution profiles

| Profile | What it can do | Default boundary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `read-only` | Inspect context and return a focused result | No file changes and no further delegation |
| `review` | Check material and identify concrete issues and improvements | Read-only; does not edit files directly |
| `research` | Use allowed search connectors and separate evidence from assumptions | Destructive connector operations are filtered out |
| `workspace-write` | Use in-scope workspace tools for the assigned work | Approvals, path boundaries, and task scope still apply |

Each member can have its own Skills, MCP, model, and thinking depth. If a selected model is unavailable, runtime capability resolution may fall back to another enabled model; avoid making a role depend on a temporary connection.

<ApprovalCheckpoint risk="high">Delegation is not an approval bypass. Member tools still follow the primary session’s access level, tool approval mode, and connector policies. Review targets and parameters for writes, deletes, commands, and out-of-workspace access.</ApprovalCheckpoint>

## Delegation modes and boundaries

| Mode | Use it when | Important limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Single | One member owns a complete, focused subtask | Useful for targeted evidence or an independent opinion |
| Parallel | Two or more tasks have no dependency | Multiple `workspace-write` members cannot run in parallel until file ownership is coordinated |
| Sequential | A later member needs the previous result | If the prerequisite fails, dependent tasks are skipped |

The lead does not need to delegate every turn. Handle simple questions, clarification, formatting, and work the lead can already complete without delegation. Members cannot delegate again; the lead must read every result, resolve conflicts, and deliver in the lead’s own voice.

<PromptBlock label="Team summon example">Prepare a verifiable competitor brief for this product review. First decide whether delegation adds value. If it does, ask one research member to collect public evidence and pricing, ask a review member to check the evidence boundary, then synthesize a one-page conclusion. Cite a URL and date for every factual claim, list conflicts separately, and never paste raw member output into the final answer.</PromptBlock>

## Verify a digital employee

Start with a small task. Check that the role follows its responsibility, distinguishes fact from assumption, uses only necessary tools, names the target path before writing, and in a team session shows delegation and a final synthesis. Only then use the role for long documents or batch file work.

<ExpectedResult>A role or team that can be summoned repeatedly with explicit boundaries: inspectable instructions, selected capabilities, non-overlapping responsibilities, and a lead accountable for the final result.</ExpectedResult>

## Troubleshooting

- **The employee sounds generic:** Put responsibility, terminology, output format, and prohibited actions in the employee instructions, then provide a concrete deliverable in the summon prompt.
- **The team never delegates:** Confirm that the task has separable expertise and state delegation criteria in the team instructions; delegation is a judgment, not a required step.
- **Parallel work is rejected:** Check for multiple `workspace-write` members. Use sequential work or define non-overlapping file ownership first.
- **A member is waiting for approval:** Open its approval card and review the connector, path, parameters, and overwrite behavior. Do not hide an unclear responsibility behind broader access.
